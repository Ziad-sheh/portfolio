# Portfolio conversation & coverage audit

Researched 8 September 2026. All 17 selected projects were searched by campaign name, brand, relevant film titles and Arabic/English terms, with targeted LinkedIn, YouTube and other public social searches. Search results were checked against the actual campaign before selection.

Nine case studies have 19 selected references in the shared `coverage` component. Additional inline links document the films, participants and craft. A missing reference means no suitable source was verified in this pass; it does not mean the work received no discussion. Social posts and search indexes are incomplete, and some platforms restrict public access.

This file records research decisions. Website copy and source selections remain canonical in `work-data.js`. No public placeholders, invented reviews, engagement totals or claims of virality were added.

## Verification notes

- Wafa AlAnazi’s public repost was read in the browser. The original Georges Gerges thread and the selected Moey Shawash / Hala Al Bassar comments were verified through public indexed content. The two extracts from that thread stay below 25 words in total. The post originates with Apple’s creative director; the selected comments are attributed to their own authors.
- Smashi Business’s Short was published on 19 March 2025. The title and description were verified through YouTube search metadata and the player page. No transcript was available. The site summarises the described topic; it does not quote unheard narration, adopt the title’s virality claim or repeat a historical store-opening plan as current news.
- Mohammad Ibrahim’s post identifies The Favourite Son. His discussion of cultural detail and product integration is summarised as his opinion. The English tagline he supplies is not used as approved campaign copy.
- Natalie Hogg’s post identifies the coffee-line Health film and links its global original. Her response does not verify Arabic dubbing credits or outcomes. The One Show entry verifies recognition of the global Waiting Room campaign.
- MacRumors covers the 2021 musical. It is not evidence for the Arabic singers or for any later iPad campaign.
- Syn, Dan Drage, Nizar Sfair, Hind Shoufani and Mostafa Salameh are participants describing their own productions. Their references are labelled accordingly. No independently measured campaign outcomes are inferred from them.
- The National and Gulf News explicitly name or embed A Journey of Rediscovery. Both are used for campaign coverage, without a claim that publication proves effectiveness.

- Matt Hopkins describes ten days filming and researching in the village and names Matt Shaw and Dan Graves. Graves’ public Vimeo metadata credits music and sound design. The filmmaker also lists awards; the site uses only the nomination and exact category independently verified in NAB’s official announcement. The organiser’s 2019 winners page did not expose usable winner data.
- FAB’s original YouTube description supports the race-day context but does not name the musicians or performers. No identities were inferred from appearances.

## Older-project narrative pass

All nine earlier cases now use the same narrative depth as the Apple additions: project context, Ziad’s contribution and a campaign-specific craft discussion, followed by the work and relevant sources. Existing media, public credits and selected references are retained. Ready for More’s four challenge films use a grid with individual captions.

Ziad clarified that his role on A Journey of Rediscovery and Ready for More included scripts as a creative/copywriter and music selection. Gumball included scripting/localisation and managing actors. FAB included the idea, concept and script. Stay Home Together was his original idea, with copy/concept and music involvement. No participant recruitment, interview leadership, composer commissioning, cast names or session anecdotes are inferred from these answers.

Fourteen local films were sampled at twelve evenly spaced frames each, including all four Ready for More challenges, both Life is Simple films and both Ramadan films. Visible actions, captions and end cards support the scene descriptions. This was not a complete audio review. Music roles come from Ziad’s account; named production credits come from linked sources. Comments on storytelling and craft are editorial readings of the work, not measured audience outcomes or recorded production anecdotes. The Gumball examples remain the three previously selected episodes; no additional seasons or performers are claimed.
